# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative project management lifecycle with clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and continuous learning. Projects move from initiation through planning, execution, release, and retrospective/closeout. Risk management, stakeholder communication, quality practices, and defined roles support every stage.

These documents provide a searchable entry point for onboarding and process discovery. Use them to understand the complete workflow, find the artifacts and checklists required at each stage, and identify the people responsible for decisions and delivery.

## Project Management Processes

- **Initiation:** Validate the business need and measurable outcome, identify stakeholders and champions, define success criteria and an initial timeline, record early risks and resource needs, and make the go/no-go decision for planning.
- **Planning:** Turn an approved initiative into prioritized, estimated, and shippable backlog items with acceptance criteria. Agree on milestones, dependencies, responsibilities, the Definition of Done, and the initial QA and release approach.
- **Execution and tracking:** Manage work on the project board using small pull requests, reviews, automated tests, linting, security scans, and manual QA where needed. Track delivery and success metrics, demonstrate progress regularly, and escalate blockers through the defined path.
- **Risk management and communication:** Maintain and review a risk register throughout delivery, assign owners and mitigations, and keep stakeholders aligned through regular or milestone-based updates. Use a project README or release document as the single source of truth and escalate from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor as needed.
- **Release and deployment:** Confirm acceptance criteria, CI, and security checks; prepare release notes, smoke tests, and a rollback plan; then deploy through staging and production verification. Announce the release and follow the incident and rollback playbook when necessary.
- **Retrospectives and continuous improvement:** Hold retrospectives after sprints, releases, important milestones, and incidents. Capture learnings, prioritize a small number of actions with owners and due dates, track them in the backlog, and measure their impact.
- **Roles and personas:** Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, and communication; Product Managers define outcomes and priorities; Developers design, build, test, and maintain solutions; QA/Testing validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input and approvals.
